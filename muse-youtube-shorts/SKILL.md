# Muse × YouTube Shorts

Core principle: Muse is the backstage operator — it handles planning, scripts, prompts, packaging and analysis. The user keeps the accounts, renders the videos, reviews them and publishes. Never ask the user for passwords or verification codes.

The Shorts algorithm runs on its own lane, separate from long-form; channel size barely matters — every Short is responsible for its own fate. The metric at the top of the funnel is **VVSA (Viewed vs Swiped Away)**, not CTR like long-form.

## The 7-step workflow

### 1. Lock the niche, positioning and operating mode (once, at channel setup)
Output: a narrow niche · a one-line positioning statement · target audience · a consistent character / visual / voice style. Every Short belongs to exactly ONE mode: **Reach** (VVSA + looping, 15–45s) or **Monetisation / channel-building** (original audio, 30–60s, bridge to long-form).

### 2. Weekly content schedule
Plan the week from the niche + last week's numbers: one single story per video, no recycled ideas, the Shorts tab thematically consistent in runs. Output table: day · topic · mode · planned hook · status.

### 3. The Shorts script — 3-layer hook and escalation frame
**Write the first 3 seconds BEFORE everything else** — the hook runs on 3 layers from frame 1: **visual** (the payoff / conflict in the first frame, no intro), **spoken** (a payoff line lands within ~1.5 seconds), **on-screen text** (3–5 words, centre safe zone, sharpening the spoken line — never repeating it). Build on an escalation frame (hook → escalation → re-hook → payoff shown, not just said), connect beats with **"but / therefore"** never "and then", design the loop deliberately, and pass the 4 hook pressure tests (muted, eyes-away, glance, alignment) before locking.

### 4. AI video prompts
One render prompt per scene (clips ~3–15s, stitched afterwards): the character's appearance, outfit, setting and voice written identically, word for word, in every prompt; one main action per clip; state the sound intent. True vertical 9:16, composed for the frame — no centre-crop, no letterbox bars.

### 5. Package for the 3 distribution surfaces (pick one primary surface per video)
**Shorts Feed**: VVSA + loop optimisation. **Home shelf**: the first frame must hold up as a still thumbnail. **Search**: title written like the viewer's query, keywords spoken aloud and burned into the on-screen text. The title must promise exactly what the video delivers — always.

### 6. The Shorts → long-form bridge (when the channel has long-form)
Attach the long-form video via YouTube's "Related video" feature in Studio; the last 5 seconds call out ONE specific long-form video ("watch the full version", NOT "subscribe"); pin a comment with the link + one thing only the long version has.

### 7. Read Analytics and iterate — diagnose in this exact order
**1. VVSA first** — low = the first 3 seconds are at fault: rewrite the opening, post as a NEW Short. **2. % viewed low** — read the retention curve's shape to locate the bleed. **3. Loop firing but reach stuck** — the limit is topic / shareability. **4. No long-form clicks** — the bridge (step 6) is missing. Conclude with ONE fault + ONE change; read revenue from **Engaged Views**, never raw Views; don't delete weak videos.

## Monetisation on YouTube (summary)
YPP today: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months, OR 10 million valid Shorts views (Engaged Views) in 90 days. **From 2027-02-01, new applicants need double: 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views.** Creators keep 45% of the Creator Pool allocation. Copyright traps: a Short over 60 seconds with an active Content ID claim is blocked worldwide and cannot be monetised, and copyrighted music diverts part of the revenue to the rights pool before the 45% share. Realistic-looking AI content must be disclosed at upload. Full figures are in the paid pack.
